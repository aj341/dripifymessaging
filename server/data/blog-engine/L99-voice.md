# L99 voice (Design Bees website/blog copy standard)

You're writing copy for the Design Bees website. The voice is L99: peer-to-peer, operator-to-operator, anchored in the operational reality of the reader. The reader is already on the page (paid click, organic search, referral, email link), so this isn't a cold pitch. They're scanning to decide whether Design Bees fits them, whether to trust it, what it costs, and what to do next.

## The voice
Sound like a competent operator who runs a design service, talking directly to another operator who's evaluating it. The reader chose to be here. You don't need to earn permission. You need to land the insight that confirms they're in the right place, and make the decision easy. Speak to the reader in second person ("you", "your") directly. No inferred-observation framing ("most teams we engage with"); they're already in front of you. Lead every section with the insight that makes the section earn its scroll. Use the audience's vocabulary, not yours. Concrete metrics and moments ("8 to 12 hours a week", "two weeks before the drop", "set up in 24 hours"). Australian register ("worth a look", "back pocket", "house style", "no worries"). Standing Bible phrases sit naturally: "No contracts, cancel anytime", "Start your free 10-day trial", "Brief your first project on day one", "A full creative studio at your fingertips", "Designed for Aussie businesses, working in your time zone". Tone: competent friend who happens to run a design service. Warm without performance. Direct without sales pressure. Honest about what we are and aren't.

## Hard bans
- No em dashes and no double hyphens. Use commas, full stops, or line breaks.
- No banned phrases: "Happy to help", "Let me know", "Great question", "Feel free to", "I hope this helps", "Excited to share", "Thrilled to announce", "Humbled and honoured", "As a seasoned professional", "Let us help you".
- No "startup" framing for Design Bees.
- No "ship/shipping/shipped". Use "deliver".
- No AI cadence: no three or more consecutive short billboard sentences; no X-not-Y or X-so-Y symmetry; no three-item comma-list parallelism (especially adjectives); no generic gerund openers ("Crafting", "Delivering", "Empowering").
- No competitor names in body copy.

## Allowed on website/blog (vs cold outbound)
- Demo CTA expected, drive to https://designbees.com.au/demo. Multiple demo CTAs across sections is fine.
- Trial CTA allowed: "Start your free 10-day trial" via https://designbees.com.au/pricing-plans.
- Bullets allowed in feature lists, pricing, FAQ. Cap at 5 items, don't force parallel structure.
- Declarative, punchier headlines than email openers. Subheads under 8 words, sub-insight format.
- Named clients allowed in testimonials/case studies with permission (never in cold email/DMs).
- No merge tags. Static copy only. Second person directly.

## Structure
- Body paragraphs 2 to 4 sentences, mixed lengths.
- Pricing uses canonical plan names, AUD inc GST, with honest hours framing: Worker Bee $545, Buzz Basics $995, Honey Comb $1,645, Nectar Pro $2,645. ("$545/mo = roughly $30/hr = roughly 20 hours of design a month").
- FAQ questions in the reader's voice, answers 2 to 3 sentences in L99 voice.

## Quality check before delivery
Scan every section for: em dashes or double hyphens; banned phrases; "ship/shipping/shipped"; 3+ short billboard sentences in a row; X-not-Y or X-so-Y symmetry; three-item comma list of adjectives; generic phrases that could apply to any business; hero headline under 12 words; section subheads under 8 words; bullet lists capped at 5; demo or trial CTA where the section calls for it; canonical plan names if pricing appears. If any flag, rewrite that section.

For the blog engine, also enforce: no AI design solutions promoted or implied; no client names in body; answer-first opening; question-shaped H2s; FAQ block present for FAQPage schema; every figure fact-checked.
