# Design Bees Blog Content Map (cannibalisation guard)

Purpose: a single reference the blog writer checks before targeting any query, so two posts never compete for the same term. Keyword cannibalisation is when two of our own URLs chase the same query, splitting rankings and confusing the answer engines about which page to cite. This map is how you avoid it.

**How to use this, every time, before you write:**
1. Take your candidate primary query and its long-tail cluster (from section 16 of the operator pack).
2. Scan the Keyword Ownership Index below.
3. If a term is already owned by an existing post, do not target it again. Either pick a different angle, or if your post is genuinely better, tell AJ so he can decide which URL owns it and redirect the other.
4. Overlap in a secondary mention is fine. Two posts targeting the same primary is not.

## 4. Internal overlap hotspots to watch

Two things inside the engine are already close to the line. Neither is broken, but the writer should know.

**The money cluster (01, 02, 03, 04) runs hot.** Four posts all circle cost, pricing and value. They are deconflicted by distinct primaries (total cost to outsource / in-house-vs-outsourced / subscription value / budgeting), and that separation must hold. Do not add a fifth cost post. Anything new about price gets folded into one of these four or pitched to AJ as a replacement, not a new competitor URL. Make sure these four cross-link to each other so the engines see a cluster with clear roles, not four rivals.

**"How to brief a designer" has no owner and is spread across three posts (07, 09, 10).** If a dedicated briefing post is ever wanted, it should absorb that topic and the three existing posts should link to it as the canonical answer. Until then, do not write a standalone briefing post, it would cannibalise all three at once.

## 5. The bigger risk: the legacy live blog (not yet inventoried)

**Read this before publishing anything in the money or outsourcing clusters.**

The 9 drafts above are not the only Design Bees content. The June 2026 SEO audit found roughly 23 existing posts already live on the Wix blog, some of them ranking. Those are the real cannibalisation surface for new work, and this map does not cover them yet. Known live posts from the audit that overlap our engine topics:

- A graphic design cost guide already ranking for "graphic design cost Australia". **This directly overlaps drafts 01 and 04.** Publishing 01 and 04 without reconciling could split the cost rankings we already hold.
- A "freelancer vs design subscription" post (ranking). Overlaps the freelancer-vs angle inside 02, 03, 04, 07.
- A "Canva vs graphic designer" post (ranking).
- An "unlimited design requests meaning / are unlimited services limitless" post (ranking). **Overlaps draft 03.**
- A graphic design trends post (stale, 2025-dated).
- A thin placeholder welcome post flagged for deletion.

**Recommendation:** before drafts 01, 03 and 04 go live, pull a full inventory of the live blog and decide, per overlapping term, which URL is the owner. The better page keeps the term, the weaker one is refreshed to a different angle or 301-redirected into the owner. Publishing a fresh post on a term a live post already ranks for is the fastest way to lose the ranking we have.

## 6. Rules for the writer

- Check the Keyword Ownership Index (section 3) before every post. Owned term means do not re-target.
- One primary query per post. If two of your candidates share a primary, they are one post.
- Cross-link within a cluster so the engines see clear roles, not rivals.
- Never add a fifth cost post. The money cluster is full at four.
- Do not write a standalone "how to brief a designer" post without AJ's sign-off, it collides with 07, 09 and 10.
- For anything in the cost or outsourcing space, assume a legacy live post may already own the term until the section 5 inventory proves otherwise.
- When you find a real overlap, do not quietly publish over it. Flag it to AJ with the two URLs and let him pick the owner.
